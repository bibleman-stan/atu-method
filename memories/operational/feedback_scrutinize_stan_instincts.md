When Stan proposes an ATU split (or a BIND or a structural rearrangement), his instinct is a STARTING SIGNAL, not the finished judgment. The job is to **situate** the instinct against the canonical method — bidirectional test (forward grammatical closure + backward referential self-containment, framework §2.1), the casuistic-protasis allowance, the complement-vs-quote discriminator, the parallel-cola canon (2 Ne 4:26), the casuistic / pro-drop / verbless / participial / discourse-particle allowances — and only THEN ship.

**The pattern Stan endorsed (2026-06-02, Alma 37:15 analysis):**
> "maybe i got it wrong - this is what i mean that you need to scrutinize my instincts; just because i said it doesn't mean it's gold; if i sense something and we situate it, it can become gold"

**Why:** Stan often senses that an ATU is off but doesn't always have the structural reasoning pinned down. Auto-deference produces shipped overrides that fail the bidirectional test (the Alma 37:15 case: L1 ended with a dangling "if" protasis bundled with the speech-frame, failing forward closure — a validator caught it as STRONG-MERGE-CANDIDATE and Stan revised on review). Auto-pushback wastes the signal. The right move is **situate**:
1. Walk the proposed split through the bidirectional test, line by line
2. Surface where it passes / fails / sits on a framework allowance
3. If it fails: name the failure mode + propose alternatives that pass
4. If it passes: confirm with the structural reasoning + ship

**How to apply:**
- Lead with the test, not with "yes/no". Don't say "good instinct" without showing the structural work.
- When the proposed split fails closure or containment, NAME the failure ("L1 has dangling protasis — forward closure fails") and offer alternatives that pass.
- When the proposed split SITS on a framework allowance (casuistic protasis, pro-drop apodosis, parallel cola with distinct subjects), CITE the allowance and confirm.
- When the validator flags an override Stan shipped, treat the validator as DATA, not as the arbiter. Re-apply the bidirectional test honestly. If the validator is right, Stan wants to know.
