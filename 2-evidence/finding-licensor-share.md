# Finding — claim #6 measured: the marker license produces 1.16% of lines

**Date:** 2026-08-11. Measured by `5-machinery/scripts/measure_licensor_share.py`.

## The claim

[[2-evidence/framework-claim-inventory.md]] types claim #6:

> **"(A) does the overwhelming majority of the work"** — `[UNPROVEN]` — *"no ratio given anywhere; trivially measurable and never measured."*

[[1-method/framework.md]] §2.2 calls the explicit-marker license **"the framework's only *productive* (break-generating) licensor,"** and says it is **"deliberately quarantined."** Whether that quarantine holds in practice had never been counted.

## Result — Book of Mormon, full corpus

| | lines |
|---|---:|
| Total ATU lines | **29,762** |
| Generated by §2.2 (marker license) | **345** |
| **§2.2 share** | **1.16%** |

Per book, the range is 0.00% (jacob, jarom, omni) to 3.37% (4nephi). The largest absolute contributor is alma at 178 breaks, 1.98% of its 8,978 lines.

**The quarantine holds.** §2.2 is doing about one line in eighty-six.

## Why this number is exact, not an upper bound

The obvious approach — counting lines that *begin with* a registry marker — answers a weaker question and over-counts. Condition **(iii)** of the registry skips a segment-**leading** marker entirely (`j == 0`), handing those to the clause / yea-B / leader passes. A line starting with `yea` is therefore not evidence that §2.2 fired.

So the measurement instruments the licensor itself. In `readers-bofm/5-machinery/scripts/bofm_generate.py`, §2.2 is `_marker_split`, a single pass that runs **last, after every merge**, and is the only break-generating rule in the pipeline. Counting how often `_split_one` actually splits is a direct count of §2.2-licensed breaks.

The BoFM registry is a closed two-entry list: `yea`, `or rather`.

## What the 98.84% does and does not say

The remaining 29,417 lines come from **§2.1 together with the KEEP-AS-IS default**, and this measurement does not separate them. It establishes that the marker license is not doing the work; it does not decompose how much of the rest is §2.1 *actively licensing a break* versus the default *holding a merge*. That decomposition is a further measurement and would sharpen claim #6 rather than restate it.

## Scope — one corpus, and that is not an accident

Only BoFM has a §2.2 registry. Searching `readers-tanakh`, `readers-gnt`, `readers-lxx`, `readers-vulgate` for a marker registry returns **zero files** in each, and the BoFM canon describes itself as *"the corpus's FIRST registered §2.2 instance."*

So for four of five corpora the §2.2 share is **0% by construction** — the licensor has never been instantiated there. The cross-corpus picture is therefore stronger than the BoFM number alone: §2.1 is not merely the majority licensor, it is currently the *only* one outside BoFM.

## Consequence for the grounding work

This was measured first because it could have redirected everything else. It did not. **§2.1 is load-bearing**, so grounding the rules against the bidirectional test is aimed at the right object, and the scholarship effort in [[2-evidence/traceability-tanakh.md]] can proceed on that basis.

The unresolved problem is unchanged and sits one level up: §2.1 rests on claim #4, *"grammatical closure is a proxy for thought,"* still typed `[UNPROVEN]` and still the hinge. Measuring #6 tells us the framework uses the criterion it says it uses. It says nothing about whether that criterion is right — see [[2-evidence/finding-requirements-phase-hole.md]].
